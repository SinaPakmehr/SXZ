# Phase 04 — SXZ3 model expansion and residuals

## 1. Research question
Can models remain useful when they predict most but not all bytes, and can
storage architecture choices improve the final result?

## 2. Motivation
Exact laws are rare. A generative predictor with an exactly stored residual may
cover approximate or noisy structure, but it adds model and residual cost.

## 3. Hypothesis
Extended word, multichannel and bit generators plus explicit residuals can beat
literal/Deflate on suitable planted and selected real inputs.

## 4. Experimental architecture
SXZ3 extends the model bank and residual formats, explores alternative block
and span storage, and includes a bounded program-synthesis prototype. Every
decoder path reproduces a test archive byte-for-byte.

## 5. Mathematical methods
Word recurrences, lane/cross-channel relations, LCG/LFSR/xorshift and program
composition; XOR, modular difference, Rice/zigzag and exception residuals.
Descriptor length and residual length share the model budget.

## 6. Dataset description
Generated recurrences and noisy variants; ECG, EEG, text, audio and model-like
byte panels from the historical workspace. Public identifiers are recorded
where established; unavailable fixtures are not silently replaced.

## 7. Baselines
Deflate, Zstd, XZ and Brotli at stated settings, plus model-off and
transform-only variants. Synthetic gains are reported separately from real data.

## 8. Experimental protocol
Fit and extend a model, encode the exact residual, pack the final container,
decode and compare all bytes. Noise sweeps and ablations vary one factor.

## 9. Evaluation metrics
Final archive bytes, exact reconstruction, model/residual shares, number of
selected models, encode/decode time, and probe counts.

## 10. Quantitative results
Detailed tables are in [SXZ3 bench](../../lab/zip-protocol/results/sxz3_bench.md)
and [special cases](../../lab/zip-protocol/results/sxz3_special.json). Results
include successful structured controls and losses on general-purpose files.
The phase established model/residual mechanisms but did not show general real
data superiority. See the corrected attribution in later validation.

## 11. Positive findings
Residuals let an imperfect deterministic law reconstruct exact data; multiple
word and channel models broaden capability; controlled noisy tests can be
encoded far below backend size.

## 12. Negative findings
A model with a small local residual can still lose after global compression.
Higher-order or invented model families add selection and description cost.
The prototype synthesizer is bounded and not a general program discovery result.

## 13. Implementation limitations
The bank is finite, several models have parameter/selection biases, and
timings depend on the platform and on running experiments concurrently.

## 14. Theoretical limitations
Approximate matches must transmit both model and correction. The correction
cost is lower-bounded by the uncertainty left by the model; a wrong predictor
can increase it. MDL does not permit ignoring descriptor or address bits.

## 15. Conclusions
SXZ3 demonstrated exact residual coding and useful synthetic controls. It did
not establish a general real-world compression advantage. Later SXZ4 work
supersedes early aggregate interpretations while preserving the raw tables.

## 16. Reproduction instructions
Use `experiments.sxz3.corpus3` and `bench3` with explicit reviewed inputs; see
[reproduction](../../docs/reproduction.md). Synthetic controls are deterministic.
