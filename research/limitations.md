# Limitations

Historical panels vary in size, diversity, codec settings and use of complete
files versus slices. Synthetic laws identify capability, not prevalence.
Some archival data and tools cannot be publicly reconstructed. Candidate
caps, sampling, state budgets and heuristics prevent claims of optimality.
Exactness tests do not establish secure handling of arbitrary input.
Windows-only historical timing paths may require adaptations on other systems.
See [reproduction status](../docs/reproduction.md).

Phases 16–20 primarily use bounded regions of six recurring real files; they
are not five independent general-file benchmarks. Phase 20 adds five executable
or DLL files for a targeted replication. Structural coverage in those selected
sections does not establish prevalence across full executables or other formats.
The tensor, logic, recurrence, spectral and dictionary searches cover only the
specified families and budgets. Exact round-trip verification alone does not
establish that a forward transform implements the intended mathematics, as the
Haar audit illustrates. Value-set savings, model estimates and actual ordered
region archives are different measures. No general-purpose improvement or
impossibility theorem follows from these bounded negative results.
